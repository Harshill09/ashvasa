How we help Talk with us

Ashvasa (आश्वास) — assurance. The feeling that someone is with you, and that you will be all right.

The problem

Her life doesn’t happen in categories.

Most organisations are built around one problem — violence, jobs, schooling, legal rights, counselling. But a woman’s situation never stays inside one of those boxes.

She may need legal advice, then a safe place to stay, then a bank account of her own, then work, then childcare. No single organisation covers all of that, and each new door means starting her story over, with strangers.

Many women never knock. Others give up halfway.

The help is out there. What’s missing is someone to walk her to it.

Our approach

One trusted companion.
The right specialist at every step.

We don’t try to be experts in everything. We orchestrate — we listen, we refer you to credible organisations and services, and we stay beside you until you reach the outcome you want.

Woman→ Ashvasa→ Right organisation→ Ashvasa→ Next step→ Ashvasa→ Outcome

Not: woman → a random organisation → work it all out on her own.

1

We listen first

No forms to fill and no pitch. You tell us what is happening, in your own words and at your own pace.

2

We find the right help

We connect you to credible organisations and services, matched to what you need now — not whatever is nearest.

3

We stay with you

When one step ends, we help with the next. You are never handed off and forgotten.

Something we’re exploring

Hearing from women who’ve been there.

Trust travels by word of mouth. We’re exploring a circle of women who have found help — and who can tell others, honestly, what the way forward looked like.

Why this matters

  • It is easier to ask for help when you’ve heard from someone who did.
  • A woman who has been through it knows what questions to ask.
  • No one should have to start from zero.
Where we are

We’re just beginning — and we’re listening first.

Before we build anything, we want to understand your world: where women get stuck, who they trust, and what happens after they ask for help.

We’d like to hear from you

If you’re willing to talk, we’re curious about:

  • Who you would turn to if you needed help.
  • What makes someone trustworthy enough to ask.
  • What has made it hard to seek help.
  • What happened the last time you, or someone you know, tried.
